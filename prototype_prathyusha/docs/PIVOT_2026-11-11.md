# PIVOT — 2026-11-11: from "one laptop at a time" to "describe what you need"

## Why
The user tried the prototype and said it was almost useless: checking a claim "shows nothing" (the button stayed disabled until a laptop was picked); needs had to be entered through a form; there were no recommended numbers for someone who does not know how much RAM is enough; claims could not be about a brand ("ASUS laptops are bad at battery"); and "How it works" and "Can you trust it?" were separate nav pages. Request: simple, no-nonsense interface; free-text needs; ratings per category for ranked laptops; recommendations even with no must-haves; claim checking without choosing a laptop.

**Core scenarios (D-039) — the interface must serve all five; nothing else gets a screen**
1. **Tell me what I need.** Type your situation in plain words ("CS student, coding and some video editing, ₹60k, light, good battery"). LapFit saves it as your needs and shows laptops ranked, each with a rating for every category.
2. **I don't know the specs.** Say only what you do ("I want it fast"). LapFit recommends the numbers (RAM, storage, processor level, weight, battery) with a one-line reason each.
3. **Is this claim true? (no laptop chosen).** "ASUS laptops are bad at holding battery" → brand average vs the rest, every model best to worst, example owner words. "I want a laptop with good battery" → suggestions.
4. **Tell me about this laptop.** One laptop: its ratings, problems, sources, one click from any result.
5. **This or that.** Compare 2–4 laptops side by side.

## What changed
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Nav: Find a laptop · Check a claim · My needs · How it works · Can you trust it? | Nav: **Find laptops · Check a claim · Compare**. A footer link opens one **About** page (how it works + trust + limits) |
| Needs form (workloads, must-haves, priorities) | One text box; we show "What we understood" and you edit the text to correct us (`advisor.parse_needs`, rules, no model) |
| Must-haves you had to know | **Recommended numbers** per use and priority with a reason each (`advisor.recommend_specs`, labelled rule of thumb); your own numbers are hard requirements, ours are soft suggestions |
| Find a laptop = name search + list | **Ranked laptops** with a spec check strip, a rating (happy % and owner count) for all 10 topics, best match first. Name search kept under a fold; live lookup kept |
| Claim = pick a laptop, type claim | **Type anything**: a named laptop → the old per-laptop engine; a model line or brand → brand/line comparison with best and worst models; no laptop → suggestions; numeric words (heavy, expensive) → listing averages |
| AI reads each review slowly (25 s) so only 3 of 14 laptops had ratings | **Fast topic reader** (`topic_reader.py`, ~2.6 s per review) labels topic + happy/unhappy for every laptop, stored in `data/votes.db` so rebuilding the main DB cannot erase it |

## What did NOT change
Quote must be an exact piece of the review; duplicates count once; hardware-modified reviews set aside; sibling models never counted; "unknown ≠ good"; every claim opens the original review; honest first-run test numbers.

## Honest limits of the new version
- Brand comparisons cover the **14 Indian laptops** (ASUS 4, HP 4, Lenovo 3, MSI 2, Apple 1) plus **15 US Amazon laptops** (evidence only: HP, Acer, Dell, ASUS, Lenovo, Microsoft…). "All ASUS laptops" means the ASUS laptops we have.
- Reader: first version precision 0.89 / recall 0.46; second version precision 0.81 / recall 0.95 / opinion 68/72; third version (shipped, 4x faster) precision 0.88 / recall 0.83 / opinion 58/65, each on 40 fresh reviews. AI-written labels.
- Recommended numbers are rules of thumb; they are not yet tied to published system requirements (open question Q-028).
- Ratings depend on reading finishing (about 2,150 reviews, about 90 minutes). Until then pages show "Not enough reviews" and a banner says how many are left.
